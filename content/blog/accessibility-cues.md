Our team inherited a design system with gorgeous visuals and almost no accessible affordances. Contrast was inconsistent, focus states were missing, and mobile gestures didn't translate to keyboard. Rather than shipping a massive "a11y sprint," we treated every component like a scene that needed new direction.

1. **Cast a champion.** Each squad nominated an "accessibility director." They owned audits, rewrites, and pairing sessions.
2. **Storyboard experiences.** We recorded full journeys with screen readers, voice control, and high-contrast modes. Watching teammates struggle through our own flows was the wake-up call we needed.
3. **Rehearse fixes.** We made accessibility fixes part of our pairing rotation. Designers, engineers, and QA rehearsed together before any PR merged.

Within six weeks, we cut accessibility bugs by 60% and earned a shout-out from customer support for how "the app suddenly feels calmer." More importantly, junior devs started advocating for semantics without being prompted.

Accessibility isn't a side quest. It's the way we ensure every user can experience the story we're crafting.
